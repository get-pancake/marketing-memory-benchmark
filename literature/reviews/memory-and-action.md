# Critical synthesis: memory, evidence and action

Reviewed 22 October 2026 UTC. Public bibliographic metadata and retrieval receipts are in `release/literature-index.json` and `literature/retrievals.jsonl`; third-party PDFs and copied abstracts are not redistributed. Numbers in this note are **source-reported**, not reproductions. Review depth is explicitly limited below. All are treated as preprints unless a proceedings record is independently verified; a venue printed in a PDF alone is not confirmation of acceptance.

## Hindsight — arXiv:2512.12818

Depth: main methods, experiments and discussion read; appendices and implementation beyond the pinned README/license not comprehensively inspected. The paper separates world facts, experience, observations and opinions, with retain/recall/reflect operations. Semantic, lexical, graph and temporal retrieval feed reciprocal-rank fusion and reranking. This already covers several mechanisms that might otherwise be mislabelled a new Brain architecture. Opinion-strength updates are heuristics, not demonstrated calibrated probabilities.

The matched 20B reader comparison in the main LongMemEval table is useful, but ingestion and reflection add computation. The broader comparison imports baseline results with a different judge rather than uniformly rescoring everything. Section 7.3 contains unresolved token-budget placeholders in the retrieved manuscript. LoCoMo Table 4 assigns 83.18 to the 20B configuration and 85.67 to the 120B configuration; prose should not be used to blur them. High factual QA scores do not validate strategic recommendation or opinion calibration. The MIT core at `5ecc639c275b8bd3df9f50a29d5cc96965fe8183` is a reuse option, but its dependencies and model calls exceed the first CPU-controlled experiment. Commercial integrations have separate terms. Do not describe our small adapter as a reproduction of Hindsight.

## Zep — arXiv:2501.13956

Depth: main paper §§1–5 read; selected prompts/README/license inspected, not a fresh reproduction. It explicitly maintains episode, entity and community levels; valid and transaction times; source links; LLM extraction, entity resolution and invalidation; BM25, dense retrieval, graph traversal, RRF and MMR. Bitemporal/provenance memory and custom ontologies are prior art.

Table 1 is a strong warning about weak baselines: full-conversation GPT-4o-mini scores 98.0 versus Zep 98.2 on short DMR histories. Table 2 reports 60.2 versus 71.2 for GPT-4o on LongMemEval, an **11.0 percentage-point** difference; the paper's 18.5% is relative, not percentage points. Its latency comparison changes context from about 115k to 1.6k tokens and does not amortize all ingestion costs. The methods first say 20 relevant items, then 10 for DMR; reproduce configurations from artifacts, not a single prose number. Single-session-assistant performance decreases. Graphiti's Apache-2.0 repository at `8e320f880e7d169a907dbf2a7ac4188586c03b55` is not identical to hosted Zep or the January 2025 experiment. We borrow principles, not its headline results.

## LongMemEval and LongMemEval-V2 — arXiv:2410.10813, 2605.12493

Depth: original main methods and experiments through §5.4; V2 main paper through discussion. Remaining appendices are not claimed fully read. Both pinned READMEs and licenses inspected. The original contains 500 manually curated questions over synthesized and edited conversational histories, covering extraction, temporal/multi-session reasoning, updates and abstention. Granularity, retrieval keys and temporal query expansion matter; a weak reader can be harmed by additional context. Its historical commercial-agent checks are not current product assessments.

V2 moves to agent trajectories, workflow knowledge, gotchas and premise awareness: 451 questions, 1,540 trajectories, including unsuccessful trajectories. Its construction deliberately selects questions that frontier systems find difficult; this is not a natural task-frequency sample. RAG and coding-memory tracks use different retrieval controllers, so their cross-track ranking is not a clean memory-only effect. Within-track ablations are more informative. The shared answer reader is Qwen3.5-9B. Runbook C's reported quality/latency tradeoff concerns answering trajectory questions, not executed GTM recommendations. Its label-blind query interface is worth adopting. Neither benchmark supplies our GTM contract or commercial utility labels.

## AFTER — arXiv:2606.23127

Depth: main benchmark/protocol, selected experiment tables and discussion/limitations; appendices not fully read. There are 382 workplace tasks, six roles and 22 skills. Fixed skill annotations separate skill quality from retrieval. M1 is average fraction of tests passed and M2 is complete-task success, which must not be interchanged. Skill improvements vary with role/model; negative cross-role transfer is reported. Training gains do not reliably imply held-out gains. Figure 6 is an illustrative task's token accounting, not proof of a universal saving. The ethics sentence that all datasets were collected by original authors needs reading alongside §2.2's newly authored tasks; provenance must be checked per task. We adopt held-out family evaluation and versioned prompts, not procedural-learning claims. Artifact license/reproduction feasibility remain unverified.

## EviGraph — arXiv:2610.00212

Depth: main methods, evaluation, limitations and start of appendix; remaining appendices not fully read. This is the closest counterexample to claiming evidence-backed recommendation as new: temporal scoped evidence, critical obligations, proof checks and recommend/verify/exclude decisions are already central. The study uses 6,300 generated scenarios and 25,500 decisions with deterministic policy labels; those labels are not human judgments of service usefulness. Entity/template holdouts and entity-level paired bootstrap are useful precedents.

Its all-field policy shows how abstaining on everything can achieve low error with no coverage. The verifier removes 21 supported recommendations and no unsupported ones in one reported comparison; because false positives were already zero there, this cannot establish an error reduction. Rule-only performance is competitive in parts of the benchmark. GPT-5-mini is the principal reader; exploratory alternative-model checks were added after observing test outcomes. Internal proof validity is not real-world truth. Our possible contribution is a controlled GTM retrieval comparison under limited evidence, not a new proof-carrying recommendation principle. No EviGraph code or data has been copied; a redistributable artifact license is not established.

## MemCompile — arXiv:2610.23313

Depth: main paper and first normalization appendix, plus actual `src/packet_scheduler.py` at `118a26f7a5f4e3f19488e24e093f6bd19e855c73`; not all code/appendices. The method consolidates memory hits into source packets, selects complementary evidence with an LLM scheduler, and compiles reader views. Its fixed-packet ablation usefully separates selection from rendering. LoCoMo has only ten conversation clusters; resampling questions alone would overstate independence. A packet-count budget is not a token-matched budget, and richer bundles can increase reader input. Its accounting explicitly excludes embedding and judging.

Code inspection confirms a label-blind serving signature, strict schema validation, retries, salvage and additional selection rounds. All of this computation must be counted. Cache hits zero current token accounting while retaining origin totals: cached analysis reproduction is distinct from fresh inference cost. The README and source are public, but a root LICENSE fetch returned 404 and the GitHub tree API was blocked; this does not prove the repository has no license. No code is copied until permission is established. Complementary evidence coverage is prior art, not our novel mechanism.

## ReFind — arXiv:2608.12888

Depth: main architecture and evaluation through initial discussion; later appendices not fully read. A raw-turn BM25 index, session grouping, temporal filters, turn expansion, seen-session exclusion and a four-round search/note/finish controller challenge the need for elaborate persistent representations. Main comparisons include published scores, not a uniform rerun of every baseline. Controlled raw-history controls and retrieval ablations are more relevant than the headline ranking. On one dataset plain BM25 slightly exceeds ReFind. Zero LLM ingestion cost is not zero total cost: retrieval involves multiple LLM calls. Repeated-run standard deviation is not uncertainty over independent histories. We include a strong raw-event hybrid/diversity control; we do not claim it implements ReFind's agentic search.

## Explicit memory versus raw history — arXiv:2610.24671

Depth: full main methods/results/discussion; appendices partially inspected; README, MIT LICENSE and `vendor/VERSIONS.md` read. Repository: `ielab/memory-systems-vs-rag`, revision `d67e4b15daedd3e79bb03a6ebb9f57bf46244e5a`. It standardizes readers, retrieval interfaces and budget sweeps across six memory systems and raw-turn/user/pair representations. Eight retrievers and eight budgets show that retriever strength can reverse a memory conclusion. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is a feasible strong control for this runtime.

The full-context reference is capped at 100k recent tokens, so it is not literally all evidence for every history. A best-of-64 configuration result is descriptive and optimistic unless selected on development data. Boxes over configurations are not confidence intervals over independent worlds. Construction and query cost are recorded separately; a query-only total does not amortize construction. Full reproduction would require much larger model resources and judging access; our smaller study cannot be described as reproducing its benchmark. We reuse the comparison principle and standard RRF, with independently written code and pinned open models.

## RECON — arXiv:2607.16716

Depth: main construction, experiments and limitations read; appendices not fully read, anonymous artifact not inspected. Deterministic provenance DAGs precede LLM narration; 24 cases and 1,604 questions cover evidence chains, cascading invalidation, conflict, counterfactuals and time. Gold provenance, narrator checks and human review are reported. The main evaluation removes 190 questions answerable closed-book by two of three models: this changes the target population and must be disclosed.

An oracle receiving the dependency graph also changes representation and input length, so the oracle gap is not purely a retrieval intervention. Some paraphrasing systems are excluded from header-based recall diagnostics. Human annotators receive curated evidence packets rather than full histories; the paper explicitly acknowledges this asymmetry. A nonsignificant narrator-family test across 24 cases does not prove absence of narrator bias. For our design, preserve alternate valid supports and stable controls, but do not claim dependency invalidation as new or extrapolate long-context crime/medical performance to GTM.

## AkasicMEM — arXiv:2609.25563

Depth: main architecture, authorization model and discussion read, with a small extraction gap in §4.2; not an implementation audit. Source-memory lineage, restriction intersection, retrieval-time revalidation and audited declassification address authorization continuity. Identity of deleted sources persists in the lineage. The paper explicitly recognizes synchronization latency for external sources, over-restriction from intersecting policies, and aggregate disclosure that sentence-level NLI cannot rule out.

This is an architecture/requirements paper: §5 proposes evaluation, rather than presenting a benchmark proving usefulness or security. Outcome-driven adaptation is explicitly future work. We must not borrow performance guarantees from it. Shared causal/access filtering in our test is an experimental boundary, not an implemented enterprise authorization system or novel governed-memory design.

## MemDerive — arXiv:2610.25373

Depth: selected main construction, diagnosis, maintenance and experimental sections; not every figure, appendix or implementation. This very recent work substantially overlaps the proposed diagnostic contribution. It constructs evolving symbolic worlds, derives gold with Datalog, preserves witness sets and probes intermediate states. It distinguishes access, evolution and composition; separates refresh after change from preservation of stable answers; and tests raw BM25, dense and full-context references alongside memory systems. Query-only recall is insufficient, and missing jointly necessary inputs can dominate.

The reported 10 scenarios/1,503 queries are not 1,503 independent worlds. Its provenance-aware gold and interventions are stronger than simply checking answer words. A fixture study that adds a GTM noun to this design would be incremental. Our defensible scope is a **domain-specific controlled technical report**: an executable Play contract, matched prompt/retrieval controls and a direct test of whether domain-specific coverage is needed once a generic coverage control is included. We do not claim to invent derived-state maintenance. Anonymous code/data licenses are unverified, so no artifacts are copied.

## Sales Research Bench and CRMArena-Pro — arXiv:2602.17017, 2505.18878

Depth: selected architecture, evaluation and limitations, not all appendices. Sales Research Bench evaluates 200 business-analysis questions with eight weighted model-judged dimensions. Native Dynamics access versus a SQL MCP connector, different backbones and orchestration confound attributing the reported aggregate advantage to domain memory. Its chart and narrative quality scores do not measure Play applicability; promised release is not evidence that a package is available. CRMArena-Pro already evaluates business workflows and confidentiality with a shared ReAct interface. Explicit confidentiality prompts help refusal rates while sometimes harming completion, reinforcing the need for a prompt-only control. Its LLM user simulation has documented errors; a 20-trajectory check is limited validation. Neither provides observed GTM revenue benefit.

## Additional counterexamples and coverage limits

Abstract/intro screening only, not deep reviews: MemoryArena (2602.16313) already couples memory with interdependent agentic action; Org-Agent (2609.34392) links identity, authority and temporal knowledge to decisions; PLACE (2610.25179) separates write correctness and read eligibility; More Memory, Worse Agents (2610.27406) studies feedback errors; the driving-memory controlled study (2610.27714) emphasizes warmup-pool dependence, token-matched fillers and baseline audit. Mem0 (2504.19413) is a major reusable memory system, but its own headline results are not reproduced here. These sources further preclude broad “first memory-to-action” claims. Detailed findings will be cited only at the reviewed depth.

The 107-page memory survey (2512.13564) is a navigation aid, not a substitute for original evidence and not claimed fully read. Search coverage is bounded and cannot include every scientific paper. Remaining gaps include comprehensive forward-citation indexing, older licensed full texts, independent human assessment, and complete reproduction of large-model competitors. These limits favor a modest empirical report over a state-of-the-art claim.
