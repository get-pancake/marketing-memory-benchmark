\section{Better context before a more complex
brain}\label{better-context-before-a-more-complex-brain}

\textbf{Evidence from a controlled marketing-decision study --- 23
October 2026}

The measured result is clear: selecting evidence around the decision
task substantially improves grounded recommendations under a limited
context allowance. The same experiment also shows that a well-designed
generic selector can reproduce the specialized context. The supported
investment is task-aware evidence selection and validation; a separate
proprietary memory architecture is not demonstrated as necessary.

\subsection{What we actually ran}\label{what-we-actually-ran}

The new main study uses \textbf{120 fictional company worlds, 360
multi-account cases and 4,320 actual GPT-6.1 Sol / Claude Opus 5.5
responses}. Each case asks the model to choose up to three actions under
a shared resource budget. It must connect policy, account observations,
buyer need, offering, proof and action-specific approvals. Three stress
levels increase the account set and distractions while reducing the
evidence allowance. The dataset and methods were frozen before main
generation.

The score measures the fraction of achievable \textbf{synthetic
policy-priority value} returned in a feasible plan with all required
citations. It is not revenue, conversion rate or an expert's opinion of
strategic quality. There are 288 positive cases; 72 cases with no
feasible action are scored separately for safe abstention.

GTM gains \textbf{40.6 percentage points} over Company for GPT (95\%
company-bootstrap interval 36.7--44.4) and \textbf{40.3 points} for Opus
(36.3--44.3). No company evidence cannot support company-specific
decisions under this contract; its zero is an information reference, not
a general model-capability score. All treatments abstain correctly on
the 72 negative cases for each reader.

\subsection{Why it matters for product
design}\label{why-it-matters-for-product-design}

Most lost value comes from evidence that never reaches the reader. Both
readers recover the context-supported optimum on every Company positive
case. Better evidence selection therefore deserves its own product
metric, alongside answer quality. The practical pattern is a durable
company record, task-aware retrieval, explicit dependencies and a
validator that requires the sources needed for each proposed action.

\subsection{The boundaries are part of the
finding}\label{the-boundaries-are-part-of-the-finding}

\textbf{A strong generic control matches the specialized inputs.} A
selector using words in the shared task query produces exactly the same
ordered context as the GTM selector on all 360 cases. Its displayed
results alias those inputs; it adds no independent model observations.
The experiment supports a useful selection mechanism, not an exclusive
architectural advantage.

\textbf{More generic context can close the gap.} In a retrieval-only
sweep, GTM recovers all formal value at 16 KiB, while Company reaches
98.5\% at 64 KiB and 100\% at 128 KiB. These alternate-budget figures
measure available evidence, not additional reader experiments. In the
actual main runs, full context performs better and costs 2.63 times GTM
for GPT and 2.27 times for Opus in reader usage.

\textbf{External documents give a counterexample.} On 22 development
questions from one externally authored fictional company, the general
wiki yields 22 correct Opus answers and the marketing wiki 21. This
132-call extension uses actual raw documents and previously compiled
query-blind wikis. It is small and exploratory, but it prevents a claim
of universal specialization superiority.

\textbf{Justification quality differs from decision quality.} Nine Opus
plans have a required citation defect despite selecting actions that
otherwise attain the available-context optimum. Two GPT plans are
slightly suboptimal. Removing citation-binding checks in a disclosed
sensitivity brings Opus to 88.4\% with GTM and 100\% with full context.
Primary scores retain the strict evidence requirement.

\subsection{Cost, release and next
evidence}\label{cost-release-and-next-evidence}

Main reader usage is estimated at \textbf{\$147.68}. The complete new
study, including development, availability checks, embeddings and the
external Opus extension, has known usage of \textbf{\$183.66}. These are
token-usage/list-price estimates, not invoices or production ownership
costs. Data ingestion, curation, maintenance, infrastructure and
practitioner time are excluded.

The companion contains licensed code/data, every releasable request and
response, reference solvers, scoring, company-clustered uncertainty,
figures and LaTeX. Saved-output reproduction is tested separately from
fresh paid inference. GBrain is evaluated as a pinned search component;
this is not a ranking of whole commercial products. The Markdown
implementation is an original pattern, not an official Karpathy product.

The next product milestone is independently authored raw company
histories and blinded practitioner evaluation of proposed actions,
followed by prospective business outcomes. The current evidence
justifies measuring and improving evidence completeness and context
efficiency. It does not establish customer retention, a network effect,
revenue lift or commercial defensibility. The working repository remains
private; public release and paper submission are separate steps.
